50/50 Genius
Alice Xiao
One ritual I have when I need to win a 50/50 in situations such as flipping a coin, playing Minesweeper, or gambling in general is that I would say something like, “Based on my genius analysis, the coin is going to land on heads.” When I say my phrase, I believe there is a good chance that I will be right. Saying this phrase has supposedly helped me win many Minesweeper games, so I have started to trust my genius analysis even more. Before saying my phrase, I look at the choices with my genius power and think about them like I am actually doing some kind of analysis. In reality, I am just guessing and hoping for the best. For example, when flipping a coin, I might stare at it for a few seconds and act like I am calculating the laws of physics before predicting heads or tails. When playing Minesweeper, I might stare at the squares on the screen, look for patterns, and pretend that my brain is processing millions of calculations before clicking on a square. Even when there is no real way to know what will happen, I act like I have figured out the answer through pure intelligence. I also like to convince other people that my ritual actually works because I am a genius. If I predict the correct result, I act like I have just proven my intelligence to the world. For example, if I guess heads correctly three times in a row, I might start acting like I have mastered the art of predicting coin flips. However, when my ritual does not work, I convince everyone that it is because I was not confident enough in my genius analysis when I said the phrase. I never blame my prediction skills; I blame my lack of confidence instead. I think this ritual is funny because I make a random guess that sounds like a serious scientific experiment. Instead of admitting that I am just lucky, I act like I have a special power to see the future. Even though I know my phrase does not actually change the odds, I still enjoy doing it because it makes every prediction more exciting. At this point, my genius analysis has become less about being correct and more about convincing everyone that I know exactly what I am doing.
